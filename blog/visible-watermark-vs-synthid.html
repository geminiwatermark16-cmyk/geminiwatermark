Visible watermark vs SynthID: what is the difference?

A visible logo and an invisible provenance signal may both relate to AI-generated media, but they are not the same thing and should not be discussed as if one automatically replaces the other.

Updated Aug 20, 2026•5 min read

A visible watermark is part of the pixels you can see

A visible watermark is an overlay that appears directly in the image or video frame. It may be a wordmark, icon or small symbol placed near an edge. Because it is visible, a user can point to the exact area on screen.

Visible cleanup tools work by changing pixels in that local area. The goal is cosmetic: reconstruct the background as naturally as possible while leaving the rest of the frame untouched.

SynthID-style provenance is different

SynthID is designed as an invisible provenance signal rather than a normal corner logo. It is not something a user can simply crop out by removing a visible patch. A file can look visually clean and still contain provenance information.

Important distinctionRemoving a visible overlay does not mean invisible provenance has been removed. The two systems serve different purposes.

Why this distinction matters for creators

Creators often use the word “watermark” for any identifier attached to AI media. That can create confusion. If a client asks for “no watermark,” first determine whether they mean a visible logo in the frame or whether they are asking about provenance systems. These are different requests with different technical implications.

Can a visible mark be removed without changing the whole image?

Yes, when the watermark profile and placement are known. A local cleanup process can modify only the small region containing the visible overlay. The difficult part is reconstructing texture underneath it, especially when clothing, foliage or moving objects pass behind the mark in a video.

Should you disclose AI-generated media?

Disclosure requirements depend on the platform, context and how the content is used. Removing a visible overlay for an authorized editing workflow does not remove your responsibility to follow the publishing platform’s rules, client requirements or applicable law.
